GPS Disciplined Oscillator

While there are plenty of open-source GPS-Disciplined Oscillator (GPSDO) designs out there, I decided to build my own from scratch to integrate a few specific features and performance improvements. At its core, the project uses a GNSS receiver to discipline a 10 MHz Oven-Controlled Crystal Oscillator (OCXO) for exceptional long-term frequency stability. Rather than relying on a raw timing pulse directly from the GPS module, the unit generates its own Pulse-Per-Second (PPS) output derived directly from the clean 10 MHz OCXO signal, phase-aligned to the GNSS time scale via an internal divider. To safely feed multiple pieces of test gear without introducing ground loops or cross-talk, the unit features three galvanically isolated 50 Ω pure sine wave outputs for the 10 MHz reference clock. For timing applications, it also provides a 5 V TTL-compatible 50 Ω PPS output. An integrated 3.3 V bias tee on the RF input allows direct compatibility with standard, low-cost active GPS antennas, and the entire system is designed for autonomous, 24/7 continuous operation powered by a simple 12 V DC supply.

Like most GPSDOs, this project will discipline an Oven-Controlled Crystal Oscillator (OCXO) to provide a stable and accruate reference frequency of 10MHz, but it also provides a pulse-per-second (PPS) signal that is aligned with the GNSS time scale, that is dervived from the internal 10MHz OCXO.

The unit features three galvanically isolated 50Ω pure sine wave 10MHz outputs, and a 5V TTL compatible PPS output, again with a 50Ω souce impedance. It can be used with an inexpensive active GPS antenna (a supply voltage of 3.3V is provided at the antenna input).

It is designed to run permanently and autonomously from a 12V power brick
